# Pre-Sale Ask Language

Patterns for asking buyers to commit cash before a product, service, or feature exists.

The pre-sale is the most useful proof test. It produces rung 7 evidence (deposit / payment / signed pilot) and forces the user to define exactly what they are selling.

## The Anatomy of a Pre-Sale Ask

A clean pre-sale ask names six things:

1. **What** the buyer gets (one sentence).
2. **By when** (a specific delivery date, not a range).
3. **For how much** (a price, in money, not "competitive").
4. **What happens if it slips** (refund / credit / extension policy).
5. **The deadline to commit** (a specific date inside 14 days).
6. **The cap** (a number of buyers / seats / pilots — scarcity that is real, not manufactured).

If any of the six is missing, the ask is fuzzy and produces rung 4 replies instead of rung 7 commitments.

## Pattern 1: Pre-Sold Cohort (Course / Group / Workshop)

```text
"I'm running [program name] starting [date], 6 weeks, 8 seats, $[X].

The deliverable: [specific outcome the buyer can describe to their team].

$[deposit] holds the seat, paid by [date inside 14 days]. Full balance at kickoff.

If we don't get to 5 seats by [date], the cohort moves to [next window] and your deposit refunds in full. No questions, 3-day turnaround."
```

Why it works: scarcity is real (cohort cap), kill criterion is named (5 seats by deadline), refund policy removes the buyer's downside.

## Pattern 2: Pilot Contract (B2B Services / Software)

```text
"For [defined deliverable], 60-day pilot, $[X] paid up front.

Success criteria: [one measurable outcome — specific number, by specific date].

If we don't hit success criteria by day 60, you keep [the artifact / data / capability shipped] and 50% of the fee refunds.

To start by [date], the contract needs to be signed by [date — inside 14 days]."
```

Why it works: success criteria are written before payment; refund risk is on the user; deadline forces internal procurement to move.

## Pattern 3: Productized Engagement (Agency / Consulting)

```text
"I run a fixed-fee [engagement name]: [scope in 1 sentence], [duration], $[X].

The deliverable: [one clear artifact + one clear outcome].

$[deposit] reserves the slot, paid before kickoff. Slots open [date]; I'm filling [N] of them this month.

I'll send a one-page SOW on signal. If we close by [date], we start [date]."
```

Why it works: removes the discovery-call pattern; sells the package, not the time; treats the buyer's signal as the next step.

## Pattern 4: Pre-Sold Feature (SaaS / Product)

```text
"We're building [capability] with [defined input + defined output], shipping by [date].

For the next 14 days, we're taking 3 design partners at $[X] for a 12-month annual contract — half off list price.

You ship feedback during the build; we ship the capability by [date].

If we miss the date, the contract pauses and your prepaid amount becomes credit on any other capability for 12 months."
```

Why it works: design partner framing matches the buyer's risk tolerance; refund policy is real but not immediate cash, which keeps the user solvent during the build.

## Variants by Confidence Level

### High confidence (multiple rung 6+ signals collected)

Lead with the price and the deadline. The buyer is already convinced; do not re-pitch.

### Medium confidence (rung 5 signals + qualified conversations)

Anchor the ask to the buyer's stated cost of inaction. Quote their own numbers back.

### Low confidence (no rung 6+ evidence yet)

Do not pre-sell. Run a Customer Interview round first. Pre-selling without rung 5+ signal produces zero buyers and wastes the user's reputation with the early list.

## Hard Don'ts

- Don't run a pre-sale at a discount as the lead message. Discount-led pre-sales recruit price-sensitive buyers who churn at full-price renewal.
- Don't promise features outside the named scope. Scope creep starts at the ask.
- Don't extend the deadline when commitments are slow. Extending hides the answer; let the deadline produce the kill signal.
- Don't waive the refund policy verbally. Put it in writing in the original ask. Buyers assume the worst when policy is private.
- Don't run pre-sales across more than one offer at a time. Splits attention; dilutes urgency.

## After the Pre-Sale Closes

Whether it works or fails, capture three things:

```text
Buyers committed:    [N + total $]
Strongest objection: [most common reason for no]
Weakest part of the ask: [where buyers paused or asked clarifying questions]
```

The strongest objection is the next thing to fix. The weakest part of the ask is the next thing to sharpen. Both go into the next round.
