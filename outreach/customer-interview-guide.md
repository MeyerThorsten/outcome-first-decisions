# Customer Interview Guide

Questions that surface buyer behavior, not buyer opinion.

Most customer interviews are theater. The buyer is polite, the user hears confirmation, and no real signal is collected. This guide breaks that pattern by anchoring every question in what the buyer has already done — not what they would do.

## The Two Banned Phrasings

Stop asking these. They produce rung 1 evidence.

- ❌ "**Would** you...?"
- ❌ "**Do you think** you would...?"

Replace with:

- ✅ "When was the **last time** you...?"
- ✅ "Walk me through **what you did** when [trigger]..."
- ✅ "**What did you spend** on [problem] in the last 12 months?"

Past behavior predicts future behavior. Stated intent does not.

## The Interview Structure (45 minutes)

### 1. Trigger discovery (10 min)

Find the moment the buyer felt the problem most acutely.

- "When did you most recently feel [pain]?"
- "What were you doing the day this became a priority?"
- "What changed in your business that made this matter now?"

If the buyer cannot point to a specific recent moment, the pain is not active. Note the rung and move on.

### 2. Current solution behavior (10 min)

Discover what they have already paid, built, or rigged.

- "How are you handling this today?"
- "What did you try before that?"
- "What did the last attempt cost in time, money, or attention?"

A buyer who has paid for *anything* to solve this problem is rung 5+. A buyer who has never paid is most likely rung 1-3, regardless of how much pain they describe.

### 3. Cost of the status quo (10 min)

Quantify the current loss. The cost of inaction sells the change.

- "How much revenue / hours / customers / margin does this currently cost you?"
- "If this stayed exactly as-is for 12 months, what would that mean for the business?"
- "Who else inside the company feels this pain?"

A buyer who cannot quantify the cost is unlikely to commit budget. Note this; it predicts deal cycle length.

### 4. Decision and budget (10 min)

Surface authority and timing.

- "Who else needs to be in the room to greenlight a $X solution?"
- "What is the next budget cycle for this kind of spend?"
- "Last time you bought something similar, what was the process?"

Decisions are made by named people on named timelines. If the buyer cannot answer these three, the deal is unlikely to close in the current quarter — even if they like the product.

### 5. The behavior ask (5 min)

End every interview with a behavior-asking next step.

- "Would it be useful for me to send a one-page proposal for $[X], scope [Y], starting [date]?"
- "Can I introduce you to [reference customer in similar situation]?"
- "If I built this in 60 days, would you commit to a paid pilot at $[X]?"

If the buyer says yes to any of these, document it and follow up within 24 hours. If they hesitate, that hesitation is more useful data than the rest of the interview.

## Rung-Calibrated Questions (cheat sheet)

| To check rung 5 (qualified conversation)     | "Walk me through your last attempt to solve this."           |
| To check rung 6 (referral)                   | "Who else do you know dealing with this?"                    |
| To check rung 7 (deposit/pilot)              | "If I delivered [X] by [Y], would you commit $[Z]?"          |
| To check rung 8 (retention/expansion)        | "How would you measure whether this kept working in year 2?" |

## Hard Don'ts

- Don't pitch during the interview. Pitching closes the data window.
- Don't lead the buyer to your offer. Let the buyer's own language surface; use it later in outreach.
- Don't accept "interesting" as data. It is rung 1. Push for behavior.
- Don't run more than 6 interviews before pausing to look at the data. Confirmation bias compounds with sample size.

## Output

After every interview, write 5 bullets within 1 hour:

```text
Buyer:                  [name + role + company]
Highest rung observed:  [number + evidence]
Status quo cost:        [quantified, or noted as "unable to quantify"]
Decision authority:     [yes / no / shared]
Next behavior step:     [ask + their response + follow-up date]
```

After 5 interviews, if no buyer reaches rung 6+, the offer is not the problem — the buyer segment is. Change the buyer before changing the offer.
