# Cold Outreach

Templates and rules for outreach that asks for buyer behavior, not opinion.

The goal of cold outreach is to surface a buyer who will commit (rung 6+) or to remove that prospect from the list — quickly. Anything in between is noise to be cleared.

## Core Rules

- **Open with a buyer-specific reason.** Not "I help X." Start with the trigger that made the user pick this prospect now.
- **End with a sharp ask.** A specific next step with a deadline and a price, scope, or commitment.
- **Cap at 5 sentences.** Anything longer is a pitch deck wearing email clothes.
- **Never ask "would you be interested?"** Replace with a behavior request.
- **Never attach a deck on a cold email.** The deck is a maybe-generator. Sharp asks are answer-generators.

## Template: Cold Email (5 sentences)

```text
Subject: [specific buyer outcome] for [company name]

Hi [first name],

[Sentence 1: trigger — one observed thing that makes you think they have this problem now.]

[Sentence 2: the specific painful gap, in their language.]

[Sentence 3: what you're offering — narrow, paid, with a defined window.]

[Sentence 4: the sharp ask with a date.]

[Optional sentence 5: removal language — "If this isn't a fit, no follow-up."]

— [user name]
```

## Filled Example (services)

```text
Subject: Tightening your Q3 onboarding flow for ProductCo

Hi Maya,

Saw your post about scaling onboarding from 12 customers/month to 40 by Q3.

The bottleneck for most B2B teams at that growth rate is hand-off between sales and customer-success — usually it adds 14 days to time-to-value and shows up in renewal data 9 months later.

I run a 14-day onboarding-flow audit: I review the last 10 onboardings, identify the time leaks, and ship a one-page tightening plan. $1,500, paid before. I have two slots opening April 14.

Want one? Reply by Friday.

If not a fit, no follow-up.

— Thorsten
```

## Filled Example (creator pre-launch)

```text
Subject: 8-seat pilot of the positioning cohort

Hi Carlos,

You posted last week about your re-positioning struggle for the agency.

I'm running a 6-week cohort for solo agencies in your situation — 8 seats, $2,000, starting in 14 days. The deliverable is a one-page positioning your buyers can repeat.

Five seats are filled with people from your network. Want one of the last three?

$500 deposit by Friday holds the seat. Full balance at kickoff.

— Thorsten
```

## Channel-Specific Variants

### LinkedIn DM (3 sentences)

```text
[Trigger sentence — one specific thing observed in their feed/profile.]
[The narrow paid offer + window.]
[The ask with a date.]
```

LinkedIn cuts attention faster than email. No throat-clearing.

### Twitter / X DM

Same structure as LinkedIn. Add: never lead with "love your work." It is rung 2 noise and signals the user has not read the prospect's content recently.

### Voice / Loom (45-second video)

```text
0:00-0:10  — Name the trigger.
0:10-0:25  — Name the gap, in their language.
0:25-0:40  — Name the offer + price + window.
0:40-0:45  — The ask with a date.
```

Use voice when the user has a credibility advantage they cannot show in text (industry, role, prior result).

## Hard Don'ts

- Don't say "just checking in." Send a new ask or remove the contact.
- Don't say "circling back." Same problem.
- Don't say "would love your thoughts" — that is asking for opinion. Asking for opinion is rung 1 outreach.
- Don't include 3 alternative offers in one message. One message, one ask.
- Don't apologize for reaching out. The reach-out either earned the time or it didn't; apology does not earn it.

## After Sending

Track every message in the Cash Proof Sprint outreach log. Rate every reply by Evidence Ladder rung. Stop counting at rung 6+. Anything below that is signal-to-design-the-next-test, not signal-to-celebrate.
