# Industry Overlay: Hardware / Physical Products

Load when the user makes, manufactures, or sells physical goods — consumer hardware, DTC products, industrial equipment, food and beverage, or anything with inventory and COGS.

Apply on top of the parent skill's defaults. Replace the generic Worth Filter and Cash Proof Sprint inputs with the signals below where applicable.

## Unit of Value

**Contribution margin per unit after fully loaded COGS, weighted by reorder or sell-through rate.**

Not revenue, not units shipped, not preorder count. Physical products carry costs software founders forget: freight, duties, returns, storage, payment fees, damage, support. A product with 60% gross margin on the spreadsheet and 12% after the container lands is a different business — and many are negative without knowing it.

## Most-Believed-But-Wrong Narrative

**"We need to get into retail / scale production to bring unit costs down."**

Usually false before direct demand is proven. Scale fixes cost; it does not create demand. The real problem is usually one of:

- **Sell-through unknown.** Units shipped to channels are counted as sold; nobody tracks what reaches end customers.
- **Margin stack ignorance.** Distributor, retailer, and freight take their cuts and the remaining margin can't fund acquisition.
- **One-product dependency.** No reorder behavior, so every sale must be re-acquired at full CAC.
- **MOQ gambling.** Committing to minimum order quantities sized by supplier pressure, not by proven weekly demand.

Prove full-price direct demand and reorder behavior before any scale commitment.

## Buyer-Behavior Signals (rung 6+)

1. **Full-price purchase without a discount code.** Customers buy at list through the direct channel — not via influencer codes or launch discounts that hide true willingness to pay.
2. **Reorder or attachment purchase.** A customer buys again — consumable refill, second unit, accessory — within the natural usage cycle.
3. **Channel partner reorders from sell-through.** A retailer or distributor places a second order because the first sold out, with the data to show it.

## Hardest-to-Fake Proof Test

**Paid preorder at full price with a stated ship date, against a small fixed batch.**

Offer the next production batch (50–200 units) for full-price preorder with a real ship date and refund right. Drive 500–1,000 qualified visitors to it. If the batch sells 30%+ at full price, demand justifies the MOQ. If it needs a 40% discount or an influencer push to move, the price or the product is wrong — and a container of inventory will not fix it.

## Common Hardware Anti-Patterns to Watch

- **Crowdfunding-as-validation.** Treating a discounted, hype-driven campaign as proof of repeatable full-price demand.
- **Inventory as ambition.** Ordering 5,000 units because the per-unit price looked better than 500.
- **Engineering perfectionism.** Another prototype revision while zero buyers have paid for the current one.
- **Channel stuffing.** Booking distributor purchases as demand while sell-through is unmeasured.
- **Free-sample marketing.** Seeding product widely with no mechanism that converts recipients into paying buyers.

## Scoreboard Defaults for Hardware Users

If the user cannot pick one number, default to one of:

- Weekly contribution margin (units sold × per-unit margin after fully loaded COGS).
- Full-price sell-through rate (% of batch sold at list within the launch window).
- Reorder rate (% of customers purchasing again within the usage cycle).

Pick whichever is currently the bottleneck per Bottleneck Before Breadth.
