# Industry Overlay: Marketplace

Load when the user's offer connects two sides — buyers and sellers, riders and drivers, clients and providers — and takes a fee, commission, or subscription for the match.

Apply on top of the parent skill's defaults. Replace the generic Worth Filter and Cash Proof Sprint inputs with the signals below where applicable.

## Unit of Value

**Completed transactions per matched cohort, weighted by repeat transaction rate at day 90.**

Not GMV alone, not listings, not signups on either side. A marketplace that books $1M of GMV from one-time transactions it subsidized into existence is an expensive lead-gen service, not a marketplace. Value lives in matches that repeat without re-acquisition.

## Most-Believed-But-Wrong Narrative

**"We have a chicken-and-egg problem — we need both sides at once."**

Almost always false as stated. The real problem is usually one of:

- **Undefined wedge.** No single category + geography + buyer moment narrow enough to reach liquidity.
- **Supply is the product.** In most early marketplaces, curated supply alone sells; demand follows quality, not vice versa.
- **Disintermediation leak.** Matches happen once on-platform, then move off. The take rate exceeds the platform's ongoing value.
- **Fake liquidity.** Founders manually brokering matches and calling it marketplace behavior.

Pick one wedge and prove repeat transactions inside it before any second category or city.

## Buyer-Behavior Signals (rung 6+)

1. **Repeat transaction without prompting.** A demand-side buyer completes a second paid transaction within 90 days with no discount, email nudge, or founder involvement.
2. **Supply-side payment or exclusivity.** A provider pays for placement, accepts a higher take rate for volume, or commits inventory exclusively — supply paying is the strongest signal demand is real.
3. **On-platform rebooking of a known counterparty.** Two parties who already know each other still transact through the platform — proof the platform adds value beyond discovery.

## Hardest-to-Fake Proof Test

**Manual matchmaking at full take rate in one wedge.**

Pick one category, one city or niche, one buyer moment. Broker 10 matches by hand — spreadsheet and phone, no product — charging the intended take rate from day one. If both sides pay and at least 3 demand-side buyers return for a second transaction within 30 days, liquidity in the wedge is real and build is justified. If matches only happen subsidized or free, there is no marketplace yet.

## Common Marketplace Anti-Patterns to Watch

- **GMV vanity.** Reporting gross volume while the take rate rounds to zero or is waived to force activity.
- **Two-sided build before one-sided proof.** Building search, profiles, reviews, and payments before a single hand-brokered match has repeated.
- **Subsidized liquidity.** Coupons and free placement creating transactions that vanish the moment incentives stop.
- **Category sprawl.** Adding verticals or cities to grow top-line while no single wedge has repeat-transaction density.
- **Ignoring disintermediation.** Treating off-platform leakage as a policy problem instead of a value problem.

## Scoreboard Defaults for Marketplace Users

If the user cannot pick one number, default to one of:

- Weekly completed transactions in the wedge (at full take rate, no subsidies).
- Repeat transaction rate (% of demand-side buyers transacting again within 90 days).
- Net revenue per match (take-rate revenue minus the acquisition and ops cost of creating the match).

Pick whichever is currently the bottleneck per Bottleneck Before Breadth.
