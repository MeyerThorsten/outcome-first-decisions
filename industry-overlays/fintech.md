# Industry Overlay: Fintech

Load when the user's business moves, holds, lends, insures, or advises on money — payments, lending, wealth, insurance, banking infrastructure, or financial tools sold to institutions.

Apply on top of the parent skill's defaults. Replace the generic Worth Filter and Cash Proof Sprint inputs with the signals below where applicable. Note: financial products are regulated; proof tests below are demand tests and must stay inside licensing, marketing, and custody rules — name the regulatory boundary explicitly in any verdict.

## Unit of Value

**Net revenue per funded account after risk cost, weighted by 12-month retention.**

Not signups, not transaction volume, not assets connected. Fintech revenue is a spread over risk: interchange minus fraud, interest minus defaults, fees minus chargebacks. A lender originating fast with unknown default rates is not growing — it is accumulating a loss it hasn't met yet.

## Most-Believed-But-Wrong Narrative

**"Once we get our license / bank partner / integration, growth will follow."**

Usually false. Infrastructure is permission to operate, not demand. The real problem is usually one of:

- **Undifferentiated wedge.** The product is a thinner version of what the buyer's bank already does, priced on hope.
- **Trust deficit.** Moving money requires more trust than a landing page generates; nobody has earned the first $100 of it.
- **Unit economics under-modeled.** CAC, fraud, support, and compliance per account exceed lifetime revenue and nobody has done the math.
- **Distribution afterthought.** No owned channel reaches the financial decision moment; paid acquisition against incumbents is a money pit.

Model net revenue per account after risk before approving spend on infrastructure or growth.

## Buyer-Behavior Signals (rung 6+)

1. **Funded account with real balance or volume.** Money actually moves or sits — a linked, funded, transacting account, not a completed KYC flow.
2. **Primary-relationship behavior.** Direct deposit switched, the card moved to top-of-wallet, the integration put in the production payment path — switching costs accepted.
3. **Institutional commitment in writing.** For B2B/infrastructure: a signed order form or paid integration with volume commitments, not a sandbox key and a Slack channel.

## Hardest-to-Fake Proof Test

**Paid waitlist deposit or production-path commitment in 7 days.**

For consumer: offer the product's core promise with a refundable deposit or paid founding-member tier to 100 targeted prospects; 10+ deposits is demand, 0–2 is content marketing. For B2B/infrastructure: ask the three warmest prospects to sign a paid pilot order with named go-live volume. A buyer who won't put $1k and a date on it is at rung 2, whatever the meetings feel like.

## Common Fintech Anti-Patterns to Watch

- **Waitlist theater.** Tens of thousands of emails collected with no payment or funding event ever requested.
- **TVL/volume vanity.** Reporting gross flows while net take after incentives is negative.
- **Risk discovery in production.** Scaling originations or limits before the first loss cohort has matured.
- **Compliance-as-roadmap.** Quarters spent on licensing for a product no funded buyer has validated.
- **Incumbent-parity feature race.** Matching bank features instead of owning one underserved money moment.

## Scoreboard Defaults for Fintech Users

If the user cannot pick one number, default to one of:

- Weekly net revenue per funded account (revenue minus risk and servicing cost).
- Funded-account activation rate (% of signups that fund or transact within 14 days).
- 12-month retention of funded accounts (or production-volume retention for B2B).

Pick whichever is currently the bottleneck per Bottleneck Before Breadth.
