# Industry Overlay: B2B

Load when the user sells to businesses with multi-stakeholder buying and contracts above $10k.

This overlay applies to enterprise SaaS, B2B services, vertical software, and tech-enabled B2B offerings. For low-ticket SaaS see `saas.md`; for agency services see `services-agency.md`.

## Unit of Value

**Closed-won ARR per qualified opportunity, weighted by month-12 retention.**

Pipeline is not revenue. A 7-figure pipeline with a 5% close rate is a $50k business with a marketing budget. Compare conversion at every stage and whether closed deals stay closed.

## Most-Believed-But-Wrong Narrative

**"We need more SQLs."**

Usually false. The real bottleneck is usually one of:

- **Buyer-champion mismatch.** The user is selling to the wrong role inside the buying account.
- **Sales cycle drift.** Deals stall in proposal stage because the economic buyer was never engaged.
- **Procurement surprise.** Late-stage procurement, security, or legal blockers added 60+ days that were not scoped.
- **Implementation drag.** Closed-won revenue does not become realized revenue because the buyer cannot deploy.

Audit conversion rate by stage and time-in-stage before approving any "more SQLs" project.

## Buyer-Behavior Signals (rung 6+)

1. **Procurement form filled within 30 days of first call.** A real buyer moves the paperwork fast; a curious buyer does not.
2. **Multi-stakeholder participation.** The economic buyer, technical evaluator, and end user all show up to a single call before contract.
3. **Reference offered, not requested.** A closed customer offers to take a reference call from a named prospect without the user asking.

## Hardest-to-Fake Proof Test

**Three letters of intent at full list price, with named economic buyers, before the build is complete.**

Get three buying-authority signatures on a non-cancelable LOI committing to a defined contract value, contingent on shipping the defined capability by a stated date. Names, titles, and email signatures — not departmental.

If three economic buyers sign LOIs, the demand is real and the build is justified. If only end-users or champions sign, the demand is curiosity, not commitment.

## Common B2B Anti-Patterns to Watch

- **Champion-only sales.** Deals close on champion enthusiasm, then die when the economic buyer asks "why this, why now."
- **Pricing the way the buyer asks for.** Anchoring to the buyer's current spend rather than the buyer's outcome value.
- **POC theater.** Free pilots that the buyer never deploys; "great signal" that produces no contract.
- **Logo selling.** Optimizing for named-customer logos over revenue or retention.
- **RFP chasing.** Responding to RFPs already wired for an incumbent.

## Scoreboard Defaults for B2B Users

Default to one of:

- Closed-won ARR per quarter (at full list, with annual prepay discounts capped).
- Stage-2 to closed-won conversion rate.
- Average days from SQL to closed-won.
- Net revenue retention at month 12.

Pick whichever is currently the bottleneck.
