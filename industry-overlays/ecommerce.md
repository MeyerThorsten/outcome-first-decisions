# Industry Overlay: E-commerce

Load when the user sells physical or digital goods through a storefront, marketplace, or DTC channel.

## Unit of Value

**Contribution margin per order, weighted by 12-month repeat rate.**

Revenue alone is misleading. A $100 order that costs $90 to acquire and fulfill is not a sale; it is a marketing experiment. Compare contribution margin (revenue minus COGS, fulfillment, shipping, returns, and acquisition cost) and how many of those buyers come back without paid acquisition.

## Most-Believed-But-Wrong Narrative

**"We need more ad spend."**

Usually false until margin, AOV, and repeat rate are sharpened. More spend on a leaky funnel multiplies the leak. The real bottleneck is usually one of:

- **Acquisition cost above contribution margin.** Every new customer loses money.
- **No repeat path.** First purchase is the last purchase. LTV ≈ AOV.
- **Wrong product mix.** Hero SKU is not what scales; the cross-sell or upsell path is missing.
- **Returns or refunds eating margin.** Dashboards report revenue net of returns; decisions get made on gross revenue.

Audit unit economics before approving any spend increase.

## Buyer-Behavior Signals (rung 6+)

1. **Second purchase within 60 days, organic.** Buyer returns without a discount push, retargeting ad, or "we miss you" email driving them.
2. **Subscription opt-in at full price.** Buyer chooses a recurring delivery option without a first-month discount above 10%.
3. **Word-of-mouth mention by name.** New customer cites a specific friend or social post when surveyed at checkout — not "I saw your ad."

## Hardest-to-Fake Proof Test

**90-day organic repeat rate, with paid retargeting turned off.**

Run the existing list with retargeting and "win-back" automations paused for 90 days. Measure the percentage of first-time buyers who return organically. If under 15%, the brand has a paid-loop problem dressed as growth. If over 25%, there is a real product-market signal worth scaling.

This is hardest to fake because it cannot be bought.

## Common E-commerce Anti-Patterns to Watch

- **Discount as default sales lever.** Every weak month gets a sale; the brand trains buyers to wait.
- **Reporting on gross revenue.** Returns, refunds, and chargebacks hidden under the headline number.
- **Influencer one-shots.** Spikes in revenue with no retention; the audience does not return without the next influencer.
- **SKU bloat.** New variants added before existing ones reach steady-state demand.
- **Subscription decoration.** Calling one-time orders "subscriptions" via a checkbox; cancelation rates above 80% in month 2.

## Scoreboard Defaults for E-commerce Users

Default to one of:

- Contribution margin per order.
- Organic 60-day repeat rate (no retargeting).
- Blended CAC vs. 12-month LTV.

Pick whichever is currently the bottleneck.
