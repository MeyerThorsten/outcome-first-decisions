# Industry Overlay: Local Business

Load when the user's business serves a physical catchment area — retail, restaurants, cafés, gyms, salons, clinics-adjacent wellness, trades, repair, or local services.

Apply on top of the parent skill's defaults. Replace the generic Worth Filter and Cash Proof Sprint inputs with the signals below where applicable.

## Unit of Value

**Contribution margin per customer visit, weighted by visit frequency over 90 days.**

Not foot traffic, not gross sales, not social followers. A busy shop selling low-margin items to one-time visitors is a queue, not a business. Value lives in customers who return on their own and in what each visit actually contributes after cost of goods and labor.

## Most-Believed-But-Wrong Narrative

**"We need more visibility — more marketing, more social, maybe a billboard."**

Usually false before the basics are measured. The real problem is usually one of:

- **No repeat mechanism.** Nothing brings a satisfied first-timer back — no list, no booking cadence, no reason to return this month.
- **Margin blindness.** Best-selling items can be the least profitable; nobody has run the numbers per item or per service.
- **Capacity mismatch.** Demand peaks are turned away while paid marketing fills hours that were already full.
- **Offer drift.** The menu/service list has grown until nothing is the thing people drive across town for.

Measure repeat rate and per-item margin before approving any visibility spend.

## Buyer-Behavior Signals (rung 6+)

1. **Unprompted return visit within the natural cycle.** The customer comes back within the category's normal interval (a week for coffee, six weeks for a haircut) without a discount or reminder.
2. **Prepaid commitment.** A punch-card buyout, package, membership, or deposit for a future date — money ahead of consumption.
3. **Local referral with a name.** "My neighbor sent me" — a new customer who names an existing one, unprompted, at first visit.

## Hardest-to-Fake Proof Test

**Prepaid package sold at the counter, full price, this week.**

Design a 5- or 10-visit package or a monthly membership priced at full margin (a convenience discount of at most 10%). Offer it in person to the next 30 paying customers. If 5+ buy, demand depth and trust are real — extend it. If 0–1 buy, the business has transactions, not customers, and the next work is the repeat mechanism, not marketing.

## Common Local-Business Anti-Patterns to Watch

- **Discount-app dependency.** Coupon platforms and aggregator deals that buy one-time visitors at negative margin.
- **Social-media-as-strategy.** Hours spent on posts while no system captures the contact of a single paying visitor.
- **Renovation before retention.** Spending on fit-out and equipment while the repeat rate is unknown.
- **Hours sprawl.** Staying open at hours that lose money to avoid "missing" anyone.
- **Untracked regulars.** The most valuable 50 customers are unknown by name, spend, or frequency.

## Scoreboard Defaults for Local-Business Users

If the user cannot pick one number, default to one of:

- 90-day repeat rate (% of first-time customers who return within 90 days).
- Contribution margin per day (revenue minus COGS and direct labor, daily).
- Prepaid balance outstanding (cash collected for future visits — the local trust metric).

Pick whichever is currently the bottleneck per Bottleneck Before Breadth.
