# Example: Portfolio Command Deck

A worked transcript of `/portfolio`. Five "projects" in, one deck out — with two rule violations surfaced, one kill, and capacity reallocated by name.

---

## Prompt

> "/portfolio Here's everything I'm running: (a) the agency retainer business, ~$9k/mo, my main income; (b) a productized SEO-audit offer I launched last month, 2 sold; (c) a SaaS prototype for client reporting I build on weekends; (d) a newsletter, 800 subs, posting twice a week; (e) a possible partnership with a web-design studio — we've had three calls. I have maybe 50 hours a week. Scoreboard number is monthly revenue, currently $9.8k, target $15k by October."

## Response

**The deck.**

| # | Bet | Outcome it targets | Segment | Rung | Scoreboard Δ (30d) | Capacity/wk | Kill date | Kill criterion | Status |
| - | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Agency retainers | monthly_revenue | B2B service firms | 8 | +$0 (flat) | 30h | — (core) | 2+ clients churn in a quarter | open |
| 2 | SEO-audit product | monthly_revenue | Same firms, pre-retainer | 7 | +$800 | 4h | Jul 15 | <6 sold by kill date | open |
| 3 | Reporting SaaS | monthly_revenue (someday) | "Agencies like mine" | 1 | $0 | 8h | none written | none written | **drift** |
| 4 | Newsletter | qualified_demand | Unclear | 2 | $0 | 5h | none written | none written | **drift** |
| 5 | Studio partnership | monthly_revenue | Studio's client base | 3 | $0 | 2h | Jun 24 | <2 named referrals | open |

**Sanity-check results.**

- ✗ **Unproven-bet cap violated.** Three bets below rung 5 (SaaS, newsletter, partnership) — the cap for a solo operator is one. Two must pause or convert.
- ✗ **Two rows have no kill date.** The SaaS and the newsletter are drift with names, not bets.
- ✓ Capacity sums to 49h against a stated 50.
- ✗ **Inverted funding.** The rung-7 audit product gets 4h/week while the rung-1 SaaS gets 8h.

**Portfolio verdict.** Overcommitted at the bottom, starved at the top. The portfolio's best evidence (audit product, rung 7, paying buyers in week one) is the least funded bet on the deck. One correction: flip the SaaS hours into the audit product.

**Per-bet verdicts.**

1. **Agency retainers — keep.** Rung 8, pays for everything. Flat is acceptable while bet 2 scales; revisit pricing at the next renewal.
2. **SEO-audit product — keep and double.** Two full-price sales in 30 days at 4h/week is the strongest signal on the deck. Capacity goes from 4h → 12h: outreach list, delivery template, a second price tier.
3. **Reporting SaaS — kill as a build, keep as a question.** Rung 1 after months of weekends: no named buyer, no presale, no kill date — it survives on being enjoyable. Capture the learning, stop the build. The audit product is the cheaper test of the same thesis: if 10+ audit buyers ask "is there a tool for this?", the SaaS reopens as a presale, not a prototype.
4. **Newsletter — change, don't kill.** 5h/week for zero attributable demand is a hobby at this volume. Cut to one post/week (2h) and every post ends with the audit-product ask. Kill date Sep 1: <3 audit sales attributed by then → archive.
5. **Studio partnership — keep, with the existing clock.** Three calls is rung 3 (conversations, no commitment). The Jun 24 kill date stands: fewer than 2 named referrals → stop scheduling calls.

**Capacity reallocation.**

```text
Freed:        SaaS build (8h) + newsletter cut (3h) = 11h/week
Reallocated:  Audit product +8h (outreach, delivery, tier 2)
              Buffer +3h — protected, not pre-spent
```

**This week's proof focus.** The audit product's repeatability: 25 outreach messages to the retainer-adjacent segment, full price, no discount. 4+ sales by Jul 15 turns it into the engine that closes the $5.2k gap.

**Next three actions, today.**

1. Write the kill entry for the SaaS in the decision journal — thesis, what rung-1 evidence cost, the reopen condition. 20 minutes.
2. Build the 25-name outreach list for the audit offer from past proposals and retainer-client lookalikes.
3. Send the first 5 messages before end of day.

**Next deck review:** Friday, with the weekly review.

---

## What the deck changed

Asked one at a time, every one of these five would have survived — each has a story. On one table, the portfolio rules did the deciding: the cap exposed three unproven bets running at once, the empty kill-date cells named the drift, and the funding inversion (8h to rung 1, 4h to rung 7) was visible in a single column read. The user did not need better judgment — they needed the picture.
