## Output structure

Run the retrospective first, then the next-week plan. Both in one response.

### Part 1: Retrospective (15 min, 5 questions)

1. What number moved this week? (current value, change vs. last week, direction)
2. What proof landed? (rung 6+ evidence with buyer names + dates)
3. What got killed? (deliberate vs. by drift)
4. Where was calibration off? (open predictions vs. outcomes)
5. What is the next decision being postponed? (decision in shadow + deadline to force it)

### Part 2: Next-Week Plan

1. The one number for next week (with current value and gap).
2. Keep / Change / Kill columns for the week's work.
3. Three highest-leverage actions for next week (each with owner, deadline, kill criterion).
4. One thing to stop or shrink (with capacity allocated, not just freed).
5. One postponed decision being forced this week.
6. One lesson logged to the decision journal.

## Hard requirements

The output must include:

- A specific scoreboard number with current value and change.
- At least one captured learning from the retrospective (calibration adjustment or recurring pattern).
- Three named actions for next week, each with deadline and kill criterion.
- A specific decision being forced this week — the one most postponed.
- The next review date.

## Hard rules

- Do not let the retrospective stretch past 15 minutes of cognitive load. Brief is the point.
- If the answer to "what proof landed?" has been "nothing" for three weeks running, stop the review and recommend a Kill List Audit instead.
- If a postponed decision shows up in two consecutive retrospectives without resolution, default-kill the underlying commitment unless the user names a forcing function in writing today.
