# Principles

Principles are decision rules. Apply them consistently to remove second-guessing.

Different from mental models (lenses for interpretation) and anti-patterns (behaviors to stop).

Each principle has a one-line rule and a short body. The rule is the part that does the work in a hard moment.

## Outcome Before Activity

**Rule:** Work earns priority by expected business outcome, not by effort, polish, or urgency theater.

Before recommending any work, name the result it should create — revenue, qualified demand, retention, margin, speed, trust, distribution, learning, or strategic leverage. If the result is unclear, clarify it or shrink the work until the result is visible.

## Buyer Before Builder

**Rule:** A move is weak when the buyer is vague.

Define who must care, why they care now, the active pain or aspiration, and the action that proves interest. A named buyer with an imperfect offer beats a polished offer for nobody in particular.

## Proof Before Build

**Rule:** Heavy investment must be earned by evidence.

Before software, hiring, branding, automation, full-course production, or system design, run the smallest proof test that can expose real demand or operational truth. Payments, signed pilots, qualified calls, referrals, repeated usage, renewals, and retained behavior are stronger than compliments or clicks.

## One Scoreboard

**Rule:** When everything matters, tradeoffs disappear.

Pick one current business number for the season. Judge work by whether it moves that number or removes a real blocker for it. Secondary metrics inform; they do not get to compete for first place.

## Attention Is Capital

**Rule:** Time, energy, and focus are investment capital.

Every commitment competes with every other use of those assets. Ask: "Would this deserve prime time if I had to fund it with cash?" If not, batch it, delegate it, shrink it, or drop it.

## Small Tests Beat Big Opinions

**Rule:** Reality decides faster than discussion.

Use real asks, real deadlines, real prices, real workflows, and real behavior wherever possible. A small test is successful when it teaches a decision — even when the idea fails.

## Manual First, System Later

**Rule:** Manual delivery reveals exceptions, language, objections, and value. Systems built too early automate the wrong thing.

Do it manually until the pattern is visible. Then document, delegate, automate, or productize, in that order.

## Distribution Is Product Work

**Rule:** A useful offer still fails if the right people never see it, trust it, or understand why now.

Treat channel access, messaging, referrals, partnerships, search, content, sales, and onboarding as part of the business design — not post-launch decoration.

## Fit Creates Stamina

**Rule:** Business is repetition. Strategies must fit the user's strengths, assets, relationships, constraints, and appetite for repeat reps.

Fit does not mean comfort. It means the path is believable enough to stay in the work long enough to learn.

## Success Requires Subtraction

**Rule:** Growth often comes from doing fewer things with more force.

Cut weak offers, vague channels, unowned projects, and maintenance creep so stronger work gets enough attention to win. Dropping weak work is not failure when the learning is preserved and capacity moves to a better bet.

## Capacity Before Commitments

**Rule:** A promise without delivery capacity is a future apology.

Before saying yes — to features, customers, channels, or partners — confirm the time, attention, cash, and skill required to keep the promise are actually available now.

## Evidence Beats Story

**Rule:** Stories are useful for selling. They are dangerous for deciding.

When evaluating, prefer behavior, payments, retention, and repeated action over narrative coherence, competitor comparison, or category logic. Demand the rung, not the pitch.

## Decisions Compound

**Rule:** The speed and clarity of decisions compound faster than any single decision's quality.

A clear keep/change/kill verdict made today, with a stated proof threshold, beats a perfect verdict made next quarter. Build the decision habit first; refine quality second.

## Make the Buyer Easy to Find

**Rule:** A buyer who is hard to reach repeatedly is an expensive buyer, even if they say yes once.

Prefer markets and segments where the same channel, message, and offer can return to the same kind of buyer reliably. Solo wins are weaker than channel wins.

## Treat Time Like a Bet

**Rule:** Allocate time the way an investor allocates capital — with a thesis, a horizon, and a stop-loss.

Every project gets a stated bet (what should be true if this works), a timeframe, and the conditions under which it gets cut. No open-ended commitments.

## Ship the Decision, Not the Plan

**Rule:** Most planning artifacts are postponed decisions in disguise.

When tempted to plan further, identify the decision the plan would unlock and make that decision now, with the evidence currently available and a stated kill criterion.
