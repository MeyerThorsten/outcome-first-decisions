# Anti-Patterns

Call out these behaviors when they appear. They often feel productive while quietly wasting business capacity.

## Polishing Before Proof

Creating logos, websites, decks, workflows, legal structures, automations, or detailed specs before confirming that buyers care.

Fix: run a proof test with real buyers before expanding the surface area.

## Building Around Imaginary Demand

Investing in a product, feature, course, service, or campaign based on what people say they might want.

Fix: ask for a concrete commitment and manually deliver the smallest useful version.

## Vague Buyer Syndrome

Describing the market as "businesses", "creators", "busy people", "teams", or "anyone who needs help."

Fix: name the buyer, urgent moment, budget or authority, and reachable channel.

## Multi-Goal Drift

Trying to grow revenue, launch new products, build content, hire, rebrand, and optimize operations all at once.

Fix: choose one scoreboard metric for the current season and make tradeoffs explicit.

## Sunk-Cost Loyalty

Continuing a weak project because it already consumed time, money, identity, or reputation.

Fix: capture the learning, compare the current opportunity against better uses of time, and stop or shrink the work.

## Channel Spraying

Posting, advertising, emailing, networking, and partnering everywhere without enough repetition in any one channel to learn.

Fix: choose one primary channel, define proof metrics, and run a focused sequence of reps.

## Feature Escapism

Adding more features to avoid sales, onboarding, pricing, retention, or positioning work.

Fix: identify the buyer objection or adoption blocker, then solve that specific issue.

## Broad Help

Offering general help, coaching, consulting, software, or content that makes the buyer imagine the value.

Fix: name a specific painful problem, specific result, and specific next step.

## Meeting as Motion

Using meetings, brainstorms, reviews, and planning sessions as substitutes for decisions or market contact.

Fix: require every meeting to produce an owner, deadline, metric, decision, or buyer-facing action.

## Automation Theater

Automating a process before knowing whether the process matters or works.

Fix: do the work manually, document the pattern, and automate only stable repeatable parts.

## Avoiding the Ask

Researching, planning, preparing, and polishing instead of asking buyers, partners, customers, hires, or stakeholders for the next commitment.

Fix: write the sharp ask and send it today.

## Low-Value Maintenance Creep

Letting admin, tooling, dashboards, formatting, and small optimizations consume the best working hours.

Fix: batch, delegate, template, limit, or remove maintenance work.

## Strategy Without Distribution

Designing an offer without a credible path to reach buyers repeatedly.

Fix: build the channel plan into the offer decision, not after it.

## Metric Decoration

Tracking numbers that do not change decisions.

Fix: pick a metric that affects what gets kept, changed, funded, or killed.

## Endless Maybe

Letting uncertain work stay alive because nobody wants to decide.

Fix: set a proof threshold, deadline, and stop condition.
