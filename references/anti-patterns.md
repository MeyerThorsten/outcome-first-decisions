# Anti-Patterns

Anti-patterns are behaviors that feel productive while quietly wasting business capacity. Name them when they appear, and apply the corrective fix.

Different from principles (decision rules) and mental models (interpretive lenses). Anti-patterns are specific habits to stop.

## Polishing Before Proof

Investing in logos, websites, decks, legal entities, automations, or detailed specs before any buyer has confirmed they care.

**Fix:** run a proof test with real buyers — a deposit, signed pilot, or qualified call — before expanding surface area.

## Building Around Imaginary Demand

Designing a product, course, service, or campaign on what people say they would want, not on what they have ever bought.

**Fix:** ask for a concrete commitment, then deliver the smallest useful version manually.

## Vague Buyer Syndrome

Describing the market as "businesses", "creators", "busy people", "teams", or "anyone who needs help."

**Fix:** name the buyer, the active urgent moment, the budget or authority, and the channel where they can be reached repeatedly.

## Multi-Goal Drift

Trying to grow revenue, ship products, build content, hire, rebrand, and optimize operations in the same season.

**Fix:** choose one scoreboard metric for the current season; mark every other goal explicitly secondary or paused.

## Sunk-Cost Loyalty

Keeping a weak project alive because it has already consumed time, money, identity, or reputation.

**Fix:** capture the learning, compare against better uses of the next month, then stop or shrink.

## Channel Spraying

Posting, advertising, emailing, networking, and partnering everywhere without enough repetition in any single channel to learn.

**Fix:** choose one primary channel, define proof metrics, and run twenty deliberate reps before re-evaluating.

## Feature Escapism

Adding features to avoid the harder work of sales, onboarding, pricing, retention, or positioning.

**Fix:** name the actual buyer objection or adoption blocker, then solve only that.

## Broad Help

Offering general coaching, consulting, software, or content that requires the buyer to imagine the value.

**Fix:** name a specific painful problem, a specific result, and a specific next step. Sell the named outcome, not the category.

## Meeting as Motion

Using meetings, brainstorms, reviews, and planning sessions as substitutes for decisions or buyer contact.

**Fix:** require every meeting to produce an owner, deadline, metric, decision, or buyer-facing action.

## Automation Theater

Automating a process before knowing whether the process matters or works.

**Fix:** do the work manually, document the pattern, then automate only the stable repeatable parts.

## Avoiding the Ask

Researching, planning, preparing, and polishing instead of asking buyers, partners, customers, hires, or stakeholders for the next commitment.

**Fix:** write the sharp ask and send it today. The polish can wait until after the reply.

## Low-Value Maintenance Creep

Letting admin, tooling, dashboards, formatting, and small optimizations consume the best working hours.

**Fix:** batch, delegate, template, time-box, or remove maintenance work. Move it out of prime time.

## Strategy Without Distribution

Designing an offer without a credible path to reach buyers repeatedly.

**Fix:** build the channel plan into the offer decision, not after launch. If the channel is not believable, the offer is not viable.

## Metric Decoration

Tracking numbers that do not change any decision.

**Fix:** pick metrics whose value would change what gets kept, changed, funded, or killed. Delete the rest from the dashboard.

## Endless Maybe

Letting uncertain work stay alive because nobody wants to decide.

**Fix:** set a proof threshold, deadline, and stop condition — at the start, in writing.

## Hiring Before Demand

Bringing on people, contractors, or agencies before the work that justifies them is repeatable.

**Fix:** do the work yourself or run a sharp test of demand. Hire after the bottleneck is proven, not predicted.

## Pricing by Discomfort

Setting price low because of fear, comparison anxiety, or imposter feelings — not strategy.

**Fix:** anchor price to the buyer's outcome, not the user's comfort. Test a higher number against real buyers before defaulting low.

## Roadmap Theater

Producing detailed roadmaps, OKRs, or planning artifacts to feel organized rather than to make decisions.

**Fix:** keep one scoreboard number, three actions, and a kill list. Discard the rest of the planning surface.

## Comparison Paralysis

Measuring against competitors instead of buyers, then matching their features, pricing, or positioning without evidence.

**Fix:** ask buyers what they paid for last, what they wish was easier, and what they would pay for now. Decide from that.

## Founder Bottleneck

Treating "only I can ship this" as identity rather than a constraint.

**Fix:** document, delegate, or remove the steps that keep the founder on the critical path of every decision.

## Scope Inflation

Letting a small test grow into a launch before any rung 6+ evidence has arrived.

**Fix:** lock the test scope in writing before starting. Any expansion must wait for the next round, with its own evidence and kill criterion.

## Trust Substitution

Using credentials, awards, design polish, or borrowed brands to stand in for direct buyer trust the user has not yet earned.

**Fix:** trade for trust through case studies, named referrals, paid pilots, or money-back guarantees that put the user's stake on the table.

## Calendar Gravity

Letting recurring meetings, content schedules, or operational rhythms outlive the reason they were created.

**Fix:** quarterly, ask whether each recurring item still moves the scoreboard. Remove, merge, or reschedule the rest.
