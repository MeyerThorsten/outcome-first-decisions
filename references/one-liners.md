# One-Liners

Sharp rules of thumb. Use one or two when concise language helps the user act.

Do not bury answers in these. They are aids, not arguments.

## Decisions

- Worth beats busy.
- Proof is the price of more effort.
- A weak yes is not a business signal.
- The market does not owe you a maybe.
- Kill the slow maybe — turn it into yes, no, or a test.
- A good kill criterion is a gift to your future self.
- A test that cannot change your mind is theater.
- One scoreboard makes tradeoffs honest.
- If it cannot win, it should teach or leave.

## Buyers

- If the buyer is vague, the plan is vague.
- No buyer, no priority.
- A specific ask is kinder than a vague hope.
- The ask is where strategy meets reality.
- A strong offer creates sharp reactions: yes, no, payment, referral, objection, or urgency.
- When in doubt, ask for a concrete commitment.

## Action

- The next action should fit today.
- Drop one commitment before adding one.
- Do the hard ask early — price, deadline, responsibility, or commitment.
- Prime work deserves prime hours.
- Choose the boring winner before chasing novelty.
- The smallest paid version is often the best research.
- Manual first. System later.
- Do it by hand until the pattern proves itself.

## Building

- Ask before you build.
- More polish rarely fixes unclear demand.
- Distribution is part of the product.
- A strategy without a channel is a wish.
- Automation should follow a stable pattern, not create one.
- Build assets, not just outputs.
- A repeatable win is an asset.
- A channel is not a channel until you can repeat it.

## Cutting

- If the work only protects an old decision, question it.
- If a project has no kill criteria, it can quietly live forever.
- If a task has no owner, it is probably not real.
- If success would not change behavior, the metric is decorative.
- Do fewer things with more evidence.
- Cut the work that only protects yesterday's decision.
