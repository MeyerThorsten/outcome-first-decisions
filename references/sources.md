# Evidence Standards

Use these standards to decide how much confidence to place in a claim, plan, idea, or result.

This skill is based on practical business execution patterns rather than a named external persona. Treat evidence by quality, not by how convincing the story sounds.

## Strong Evidence

- Money changes hands.
- A buyer signs a pilot, contract, letter of intent, or purchase order.
- A qualified prospect books a serious sales conversation.
- A customer repeats usage without being pushed.
- A buyer refers another qualified buyer.
- Retention, margin, conversion, activation, or cash collected improves after a controlled change.
- A manual delivery produces repeatable value at acceptable effort or margin.

## Medium Evidence

- A target buyer gives a detailed objection.
- A waitlist includes qualification, urgency, and permission to follow up.
- A prospect explains the problem in their own words.
- A channel produces replies from the intended audience.
- A user completes a meaningful activation behavior.
- A partner agrees to a specific introduction or co-selling action.

## Weak Evidence

- Friends say the idea is good.
- People click without commitment.
- A survey captures hypothetical interest.
- A post gets broad attention from non-buyers.
- The user feels excited but has not tested demand.
- A competitor exists but the user's channel, offer, or advantage is unclear.
- A buyer says "interesting" but avoids price, timing, or responsibility.

## Decision Rule

Use weak evidence to design the next test. Use medium evidence to refine the offer, buyer, price, or channel. Use strong evidence to justify more time, money, hiring, automation, or product development.

## Confidence Language

- Use **high confidence** when strong evidence exists and the next action is clear.
- Use **medium confidence** when the buyer and problem are clear but the commitment signal is incomplete.
- Use **low confidence** when the case relies on opinions, assumptions, or vague interest.

When confidence is low, recommend a proof test rather than a long plan.
